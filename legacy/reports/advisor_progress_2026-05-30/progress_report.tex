\section{Executive summary}

This project develops a reversible deep-equilibrium (RevDEQ) language model for the
Parameter Golf budget (16\,MB artifact, 600\,s on 8$\times$H100, scored by
validation bits-per-byte, \bpb). Unlike a fixed stack or loop of layers, it computes
its output as the \emph{fixed point} of one shared block --- so its central question
is convergence: does the iteration actually settle? We track the spectral radius
$\rhoF<1$ as the convergence criterion and add a consistency signal that makes the
model \emph{learn} to converge. In the current model the solve moved from divergent
($\rhoF=1.30$) to contractive ($\rhoF=0.77$) while \bpb{} improved monotonically to
$1.4629$ --- stability and accuracy advancing together. A reversible backward pass
keeps activation memory independent of solve depth, so deep iterations train on a
single 48\,GB GPU. Next, we will rigorously justify a convergence guarantee at no
cost to performance and benchmark against the recurrent-depth literature; we seek the
advisor's read on the direction.

\section{Problem and goal}

The goal is a language model formulated as a genuine fixed-point solve --- with
measurable, provable convergence and aggressive parameter reuse --- that is
competitive under the Parameter Golf budget (artifact $\le 16$\,MB, training
$\le 600$\,s on 8$\times$H100, FineWeb, vocab $1024$, scored by \bpb). Parameter
reuse (``recurrent depth'') is a leading route to small models, but most
implementations loop a block a fixed number of times with no guarantee that the
iteration converges; we aim to close that gap and contribute a convergence framework
that transfers across iterative architectures. All numbers here are controlled
$\sim$1000-step development runs (one variable at a time); the public leaderboard's
$\sim$1.06\,\bpb{} is a long-term target, not a matched comparison (different
tokenizer, $\sim$5$\times$ compute, and an evaluation/quantization stack we omit).

\section{Approach}
\label{sec:impl}

The model computes its representation as the fixed point of one shared, weight-tied
block. For input embeddings $x_0$,
\[
z^\star = T_\theta(z^\star, x_0),
\qquad
T_\theta(z,x_0) = \bar B \odot \RMSNorm(x_0) + \Delta_\theta(z, x_0),
\]
where the $\bar B$ term injects the input and $\Delta_\theta$ is the block update.
Capacity comes from $16$ experts in a dense \emph{soft}-routed mixture: every expert
sees every token through smooth softmax routing (hard top-$K$ dispatch would break
the reversible solve), and each owns a low-rank multi-head latent attention
(MLA, rank $64$) and MLP (rank $96$) path. The model totals $13.9$\,M parameters
(model dim $768$, sequence length $2048$, tied embeddings); ``12 layers'' denotes the
\emph{maximum solver depth} $K$, not twelve distinct parameter sets.

\paragraph{Forward solve.} The fixed point is reached by a two-state damped (Parcae)
iteration. With a learned per-dimension retention $\bar A \in [\varepsilon,1)$ and
blend $\beta = 1-\bar A$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
y_{k+1} &= \bar A \odot y_k + \beta \odot T_\theta(z_k),\\
z_{k+1} &= \bar A \odot z_k + \beta \odot T_\theta(y_{k+1}),
\end{aligned}
\]
run for $K$ steps from an initial state, with $z_K \approx z^\star$ (we suppress the
fixed input $x_0$ inside $T_\theta$). The damping $\bar A$ keeps each step a small,
stable move toward equilibrium.

\paragraph{Reversible backward and VRAM efficiency.} The update is \emph{exactly
invertible}: given $(y_{k+1}, z_{k+1})$ the previous state is recovered by
\[
\begin{aligned}
z_k &= \bigl(z_{k+1} - \beta \odot T_\theta(y_{k+1})\bigr) \oslash \bar A,\\
y_k &= \bigl(y_{k+1} - \beta \odot T_\theta(z_k)\bigr) \oslash \bar A,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\oslash$ denotes per-dimension division.
A naive $K$-step unroll must \emph{store} all $K$ intermediate states for
backpropagation, so activation memory grows as $O(K)$. Instead, the backward pass
\emph{reconstructs} each state by running these inverses from the final state ---
activation memory is therefore $O(1)$ in $K$, independent of how deep the solve runs.
The reciprocals require $\bar A$ to stay off zero (a reversibility floor
$\varepsilon=0.1$) and are evaluated in fp64 so the reconstruction matches the forward
to machine precision; gradients then flow through a short truncated window (depth
$3$). The payoff is concrete: deep solves ($K$ up to $128$) train in
$\approx38$\,GB --- within a single 48\,GB (L40S-class) GPU --- where an $O(K)$ unroll
of the same depth would not fit. Reversibility is what makes a low-resource machine
sufficient for this research.

\section{Convergence and evaluation}
\label{sec:fp}

A fixed-point model is only meaningful if the iteration actually settles. For the
solve to reach a fixed point the map must be locally contractive, and the standard
certificate is that the spectral radius of its Jacobian stays below one
($\rhoF<1$). We therefore track $\rhoF$ as a first-class metric --- not the cruder
operator-norm bound, which would flag as ``divergent'' solves that in fact converge
--- and we make the model \emph{achieve} convergence with a consistency signal that
ties shallow and deeper solves to the same answer, a target it can only meet if its
iteration contracts. Convergence is thus learned, not hand-tuned; the results below
show it improving \emph{together} with accuracy.

things the architecture relies on: that the solve \emph{converges} ($\rhoF$ and the
residual), and that the $16$ experts stay \emph{healthy} (balanced, distinct, and
actually used).

\section{Related work (comparison set)}
\label{sec:baselines}

We will benchmark against the recurrent-depth and equilibrium-model literature. The
principal comparators:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{DEQ / TorchDEQ.} The original deep-equilibrium models: solve for a fixed point instead of stacking layers. Our backbone is a reversible DEQ variant.
\item \textbf{RevDEQ.} Reversible DEQ with exact gradients and fewer function evaluations; the method closest to ours, which we extend with dense expert routing and explicit $\rhoF$ diagnostics.
\item \textbf{Universal Transformer.} The seminal model that applies one shared block recurrently through depth.
\item \textbf{Parcae.} Stable looped LMs via per-dimension damping and input injection; we adopt its damping in our solver.
\item \textbf{Huginn.} A large latent-recurrent LM with variable test-time compute.
\item \textbf{MoEUT.} Adds sparse mixture-of-experts capacity to recover the expressivity lost under block sharing.
\item \textbf{Mixture-of-Recursions.} Routes tokens to adaptive recursion depths --- a dynamic-depth comparator.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open problems and next steps}
\label{sec:next}

\paragraph{Open problems.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Effective depth $\approx 1$ / weak expert specialization.} Routing barely changes across iterations (variation $\approx 0.001$--$0.005$), so experts do not specialize by depth and the model under-uses its iterative computation.
\item \textbf{$K$-extrapolation.} Trained shallow ($\E[K]\!\approx\!24$) but must hold at $K{=}128$; eased, not eliminated, by the consistency signal.
\item \textbf{Quantization gap.} Fast proxy $1.4369 \to$ full post-quant $1.4629$ ($+26$\,m\bpb).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Next steps.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Rigorously justify a convergence metric} that guarantees asymptotic fixed-point convergence with no worse performance, and design the training so this convergence is reached by the time training itself converges.
\item \emph{Drive expert specialization across depth.} Push the routing to differentiate from one solver iteration to the next, so experts specialize by depth and the iterative computation is genuinely used (today's effective depth is $\approx 1$).
\item \emph{Enrich the Parcae damping/mixing with manifold-constrained hyper-connections} (DeepSeek mHC): replace the single damped residual stream with several parallel streams mixed by a doubly-stochastic, magnitude-preserving matrix. That magnitude preservation aligns naturally with our reversibility and $\rhoF<1$ requirements, and the extra streams are a candidate source of the differentiated state that step~2 needs.
\item \emph{Compare under a matched (baseline) setting} against the related work in Section~\ref{sec:baselines}.
\item \emph{Optional.} Sparsity and similar architectural changes, pursued only as throughput optimizations, not part of the core objective.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary}

The convergence bet is working: we adopted the spectral radius $\rhoF<1$ as the
criterion, measure it directly rather than a conservative proxy, and made the model
\emph{learn} convergence --- the solve is now contractive ($\rhoF=0.77$) and \bpb{}
improved as a consequence, within budget and within a single 48\,GB GPU thanks to the
reversible backward. The next step is to make that convergence guarantee rigorous at
no performance cost and benchmark it against the recurrent-depth literature. We
welcome the advisor's read on the direction.
